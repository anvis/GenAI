## History & Problem it Solves

Before machine learning, classifying things was a manual, rule-based process. For example, doctors diagnosed diseases based on symptoms without statistical models, and businesses guessed whether customers would buy a product based on intuition.

**Logistic Regression** was developed to solve classification problems where the outcome is categorical (yes/no, spam/not spam, disease/no disease). Unlike Linear Regression, which predicts numbers, Logistic Regression predicts categories.

## Prerequisites

Understanding probabilities, basic algebra, and the sigmoid function.

## Working Mechanism

Instead of a straight line, Logistic Regression uses the **sigmoid function**, which compresses values into a range between 0 and 1

## What is Logistic Regression How Does It Work

Logistic Regression is used for **binary classification**—meaning it predicts whether something belongs to one of two groups.

## How is it Used in Deep Learning AI GenAI

Although basic, Logistic Regression is important in AI:
- **Neural Networks:** Forms the basis of activation functions like Sigmoid.
- **Text Classification:** Used in spam detection in email filtering.
- **Medical AI:** Helps in disease prediction models.

## Real World Example

Imagine an **email spam classifier** where:
- **Input variables:** Number of capital letters, presence of urgent words ("FREE", "WIN"), sender reputation.
- **Output variable:** Spam or not spam.

Logistic Regression assigns weights to these features and predicts the probability of an email being spam.

## Explain It to a 10Year Old

Imagine you have a basket of **fruits and vegetables** 🍎🥦. You want to decide: **Is this fruit or not?** You look at color, shape, and texture.

Your brain makes a decision based on past patterns—this is **Logistic Regression**, where we predict **yes or no** based on features.
