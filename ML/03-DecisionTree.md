## History & Problem it Solves

Before machine learning, people used **fixed rules** to make decisions—think of how businesses set manual guidelines for approving loans or doctors followed checklists for diagnosing diseases.

However, real-world decisions are **hierarchical**, meaning one answer leads to another question. For example, **should a customer get a loan?** The bank first checks income, then credit history, and finally past loan records.

Decision Trees automate this structured decision-making process using a **tree-like** flow.

## Prerequisites

Understanding if-else conditions, hierarchical decision-making.

## Working Mechanism

- The tree starts with a **root node** (main decision).
- It splits into **branches** based on important factors.
- The final outcome is in the **leaf nodes**.

The algorithm keeps splitting data until the best possible decision is reached.

## What is Decision Trees How Does It Work

A Decision Tree works by **splitting data** into smaller groups based on the most important factors.

## How is it Used in Deep Learning AI GenAI

- **Random Forests:** Decision Trees form the basis of Random Forest models.
- **AI Chatbots:** Used for structured reasoning in response generation.
- **Healthcare AI:** Helps diagnose diseases by following a logical flow of symptoms.

## Real World Example

Imagine an **HR system** that automates hiring decisions:
- **Input variables:** Candidate experience, skill match, interview performance.
- **Output variable:** **Hire or not hire.**

The Decision Tree follows a structured process—first checking experience, then technical skills, then interview scores.

## Explain It to a 10Year Old

Imagine you're picking a **movie to watch** 🎬. Your Decision Tree looks like this:
1. **Is it animated?** Yes → Go to next question. No → Skip.
2. **Does it have superheroes?** Yes → Watch! No → Pick another one.

Each question helps you get closer to your choice—that’s **exactly** how Decision Trees work!
