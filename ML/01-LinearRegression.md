## History & Problem it Solves

Before modern computers, people relied on manual calculations to predict trends—economists, scientists, and statisticians plotted points on paper and estimated relationships. The problem was simple: **how do we predict future outcomes based on past data?** 

For example, businesses wanted to forecast future sales based on past performance. Before machine learning, predictions were often done using simpler formulas or intuition, but errors were frequent. 

Now, Linear Regression makes this prediction easy by finding a mathematical relationship between variables.

## Prerequisites

Basic algebra, understanding of coordinates, the concept of correlation (how one factor influences another).

## Working Mechanism

It determines the best-fitting straight line (called the **regression line**) using a method called **Least Squares**, which minimizes errors.

## What is Linear Regression How Does It Work

Linear Regression finds a straight-line relationship between **independent** (input) and **dependent** (output) variables. It minimizes the difference between predicted and actual values by adjusting the slope of the line.

## How is it Used in Deep Learning AI GenAI

Although Linear Regression is a classic algorithm, it plays a foundational role in AI:
- **Feature Engineering:** It helps in analyzing the importance of features in datasets.
- **Neural Networks:** It's conceptually similar to single-layer perceptrons in Deep Learning.
- **GenAI:** Used to fine-tune model predictions where numerical relationships are important.

## Real World Example

Imagine a **house pricing model** where:
- **Input variables**: Size of the house, number of rooms, location.
- **Output variable**: Price of the house.

Linear Regression can find a formula that estimates house prices based on historical data, helping buyers and sellers make informed decisions.

## Explain It to a 10Year Old

Imagine you own a lemonade stand 🍋. You notice that **on hotter days, you sell more lemonade**. You create a rule:
> "For every 5-degree increase in temperature, I sell 10 extra cups of lemonade."

This is **Linear Regression**—it finds a simple way to predict results based on past patterns.
