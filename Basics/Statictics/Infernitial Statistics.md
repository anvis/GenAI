Inferential statistics is the branch of statistics that allows us to make predictions or generalizations about a population based on data from a sample.

- Inferential statistics uses sample data to draw conclusions about a larger population.
- It’s different from descriptive statistics, which only summarizes or describes the data you have.

Imagine you want to know the average height of all adult men in India. Measuring every individual is impractical. Instead, you:
- Select a representative sample of, say, 1,000 men.
- Calculate the sample mean height.
- Use inferential statistics to estimate the population mean and quantify the uncertainty (e.g., “We’re 95% confident the average height is between 5'6" and 5'8"”).
